Wet Carpet Drying Help in Plano

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. For Plano homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Warning Signs

  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold on the Backing

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Stains From Furniture

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Soaked Pad Underneath

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry All the Layers

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.
