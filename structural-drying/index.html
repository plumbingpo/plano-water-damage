Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Plano Restoration Co are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Structural Drying, Step by Step

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

Using moisture meters, thermal imaging and hygrometers, we create a comprehensive moisture map of all affected areas. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Setting the Equipment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. Temperature and airflow are optimized for the specific materials being dried.

3

Daily Monitoring

A technician comes back every day, reads moisture at each monitoring point, moves equipment as needed and logs the progress. You receive updates on the drying timeline.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts
  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property

When to Call for Structural Drying in Plano

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
