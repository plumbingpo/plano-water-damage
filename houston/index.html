Drying Out a Houston Property: What to Expect

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Moisture inside walls, studs and subfloors can linger for weeks, and that is plenty of time for mold to grow and wood to distort. On each Houston job, our task is to make that happen quickly and under control.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

We take moisture readings every day and compare them with a dry part of the same building. Most homes in Houston reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. The equipment comes out when the numbers say so, not when the calendar does.

Restoration Challenges Houston Homeowners Face

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. Our crews in Houston follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. You might see trim pulling away, a discolored spot spreading or drywall that gives when pressed. Our Houston technicians scan the area with infrared and probe it with a meter to mark the wet zone. Only that section is opened and dried, once a plumber has stopped the leak.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. Our Plano Restoration Co crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear. Stop using water in the house and keep people and pets away until a trained crew arrives in Houston.
