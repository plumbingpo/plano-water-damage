Bathroom Water Damage: What Plano Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. Our Plano team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • Peeling paint or swollen baseboard on the wall next to the shower
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Leaking Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. The surface looks perfect while the framing below stays soaked.

Mold Behind Tile

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Poor Ventilation

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.
